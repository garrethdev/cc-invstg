# Varoufakis's "privatised dollar" thesis: the argument, the evidence, and where it holds
Compiled 26 Sep 2026. Sources: Varoufakis, UnHerd 19 Mar 2025 and 19 Jun 2025; Project Syndicate May/Jun 2025 and Jul 2025; "A Trust Fund for Everyone", 10 Oct 2025; The Econoclasts ep. 1 (UnHerd, 29 Oct 2025) transcript; GENIUS Act, Public Law 119-27 (18 Jul 2025); TBAC "Digital Money" deck, 30 Apr 2025; BIS Annual Economic Report 2025 ch. III; BIS Working Paper 1270; Federal Register GENIUS implementation notice 19 Sep 2025; Forbes 10 Jun 2026; trade press Aug-Sep 2026.

## 1. The thesis in five steps (his words)
1. Trump's team wants "to devalue the dollar while maintaining its global dominance" (UnHerd, Mar 2025). Tariffs push allies to unload dollars but not into euros or renminbi.
2. Stablecoins square that circle. Bully Japan or Germany into swapping dollars for dollar stablecoins; the issuer buys Treasuries with the proceeds; "the dollar will have devalued while demand for long-term US government debt will rise." He read this into Stephen Miran's Mar-a-Lago paper (Econoclasts).
3. The GENIUS Act "weaponises stablecoins as a means of privatising money and effectively outsourcing dollar dominance to Trump-friendly techlords" (UnHerd, Jun 2025). "This isn't innovation; it's a hostile takeover of money itself ... a Trojan horse for the full privatisation of money" (PS, Jun 2025). His label: "monetary feudalism."
4. Five systemic risks: issuer moral hazard; deposit migration steepening the yield curve; a doom loop between stablecoins and banks (Circle's USD 3.3bn at SVB, 2023); a doom loop between stablecoins and tokenised securities; global fragility from offshore dollar stablecoins no Fed will rescue.
5. The state has disarmed itself. The Fed is barred from a digital dollar and given no stablecoin equivalent of the FDIC. "It's a bit like taking the shock absorbers out of your car and driving it into a pothole" (Econoclasts).

His alternative: a Fed wallet for everyone, "the mother of all stablecoins," paying the policy rate with free payments; then a "Monetary Commons" in which the central bank offsets the shrinking money multiplier by crediting a dividend to every wallet. He puts the dividend at USD 213 trillion, "more than USD 600 thousand for each woman, man and child" (Oct 2025).

## 2. Where he is on solid ground
- The GENIUS Act does what he says on the essentials. It makes payment-stablecoin issuance a licensed activity, requires 1:1 reserves in cash, T-bills and similar, bans rehypothecation, and bans issuers paying interest (sec. 4(a)(11)). Big Tech and public companies can issue only through the Act's financial-activity gate. Treasury's own implementation notice confirms the two-tier state/federal structure.
- The deposit-flight number is real, with a caveat. The Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee's 30 Apr 2025 deck identified about USD 6.6 trillion of transactional deposits as the tier most exposed to migration, and cited a USD 2 trillion stablecoin market by 2028. The ABA and ICBA now use the same figure to lobby against yield-bearing stablecoins. Forbes (Jun 2026): "the deposit-flight question is no longer hypothetical."
- The Treasury-demand channel is real. BIS Working Paper 1270 (May 2025), which he cites, finds stablecoin issuers bought roughly USD 40bn of T-bills in 2024 and that inflows measurably lower 3-month bill yields. Issuers are now among the largest bill buyers.
- The BIS itself agrees on the risks. Its June 2025 Annual Economic Report says stablecoins "perform poorly when assessed against the three tests" of singleness, elasticity and integrity, and "fall short of requirements to be the mainstay of the monetary system." It prefers tokenised central bank reserves and bank deposits on a unified ledger. That is institutionally the same worry Varoufakis has, minus the politics.
- The Fed is disarmed on the retail side. EO 14178 (Jan 2025), Powell's one-word "yes" that there would be no CBDC on his watch, Warsh's April 2026 testimony, and the ROAD to Housing Act's ban on a distributed-ledger CBDC through 2030 all confirm it. No public digital dollar is coming this decade.
- The Circle/SVB episode happened as described. USDC broke its peg in March 2023 until the deposit backstop was announced.
- Direction of travel since he wrote: a 21-bank consortium (Goldman, Citi, BofA, Wells and others) announced on 1 Sep 2026 a shared dollar stablecoin company for 2027; JPM Coin, Citi Token Services and sofiUSD are live; 39 state banking associations launched the BankChain Alliance (Aug 2026) so community banks are not cut out; U.S. Bank put a stablecoin live on Stellar in Sep 2026; Goldman and Citi analysts forecast USD 3-4 trillion of stablecoins by 2030 from under USD 300bn today. Money creation is migrating to licensed private tokens exactly as he predicted, though so far to banks more than to Big Tech.

## 3. Where he overstates or gets details wrong
- The state-regulation threshold is USD 10 billion, not the "USD 50bn" he gave in UnHerd. Above it, issuers move to federal supervision (OCC or Fed). The Act is stricter than he described.
- GENIUS does not ban a Fed digital dollar. That came from the executive order, the Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act (House-passed) and finally the housing law. He conflated three instruments. The conclusion stands; the attribution does not.
- The interest ban cuts against his deposit-flight story. Because holders earn nothing, GENIUS stablecoins are a poor substitute for savings accounts. TBAC's own deck distinguishes non-yielding coins (growth tied to payments utility) from yield-bearing designs ("potential rotation from traditional deposits"). The USD 6.6tn is the pool "at risk", not a forecast of what moves. He upgraded a scenario to a prediction, and his Monetary Commons dividend is computed off that upgrade.
- "Big Tech will own the dollar" has not happened yet. The Act's financial-activities test and the banks' head start mean the 2026 issuers are JPMorgan, Citi, SoFi, U.S. Bank and a Wall Street consortium. His worry about Wall Street rents applies; the "techlords" framing so far does not. Tether remains offshore and outside the US regime.
- The Japan mechanism is speculation. No evidence exists that Tokyo or Berlin has been pushed into stablecoins. Reserve managers have bought gold, not USDT.
- The Trust Fund arithmetic is wrong on its face. A 33x multiplier (USD 3 to USD 100) is not how modern money works; broad money is roughly 4-5x base money in the US, and banks are not reserve-constrained. USD 213 trillion is about seven times US M2. A central bank crediting that sum to wallets to "keep the money supply constant" would not keep it constant. The proposal's political point survives; the number does not.
- Bretton Woods analogy is loose. Fully reserved narrow money (which GENIUS mandates and audits) is structurally different from a fractionally backed gold peg. The historical parallel is money-market funds in 2008, which is a real precedent but a less catastrophic one.
- Münchau's pushback in the same episode is worth keeping: crypto is not one thing; Bitcoin and the blockchains would survive a stablecoin bust; and the exposure is partly a European choice, since Europe declined to make the euro a rival reserve currency.

## 4. The genuinely open questions his critics cannot dismiss
1. Who backstops an offshore dollar stablecoin run? Nothing in GENIUS reaches Tether's Salvadoran balance sheet, and no swap line covers European holders of USDT. The ECB has said this out loud.
2. What happens to Treasury bill markets in a stablecoin run? If issuers must dump USD 1-2 trillion of bills in a week, the 2020 dash-for-cash is the template, and the Fed would be forced in as buyer of last resort for an asset class Congress told it not to touch.
3. Fiscal capture. Once the Treasury relies on stablecoin bill demand to hold down yields, regulators acquire an incentive not to shrink the sector. That is the "secret enabler of more deficit spending" point, and it is structural rather than partisan.
4. The two-system split. Public digital money in China, India and possibly the euro area; private dollar tokens everywhere else. He calls this geo-monetary bifurcation. The BIS's unified-ledger agenda and Europe's digital euro are attempts to occupy the public side before the private side wins.

## 5. Bottom line
The direction of his diagnosis is correct and shared by the BIS, the ECB and the US banking lobby: the United States has chosen to route the digital dollar through licensed private issuers and has legally foreclosed the public alternative until 2031. His specifics are uneven. The threshold, the attribution of the CBDC ban, the "prediction" of USD 6.6tn, the Japan story and the USD 213tn dividend do not survive checking. The strongest version of his case is the one he made in the Econoclasts: stablecoins have become macroeconomically significant, they concentrate Treasury-market and bank-run risk in entities without a lender of last resort, and the legislation removed the shock absorbers before the pothole.
