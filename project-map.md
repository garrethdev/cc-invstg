## 4. THE ROLLOUT = THE META MOVIE (top priority, owner 2026-10-07)
The rollout of the series is the most important thing. The rollout itself is the movie: the hunt for whistleblowers, the masked host spilling into the real world, institutions reacting. Make the series, film the making of it, and the feature is the story of all of it.

**Precedents:** Borat (in-character press tour), Nathan Fielder's *The Rehearsal* (the making is the show), *Exit Through the Gift Shop* (a film about a film that becomes a different film), *Catfish* (an investigation that turned into the film), Blair Witch and The Dark Knight's "Why So Serious" ARG (rollout as fiction in the real world), Joaquin Phoenix's *I'm Still Here* (warning: the hoax backfired when audiences felt tricked).

**Rollout sketch (draft):**
0. **Now: capture everything.** Behind-the-scenes cameras on the investigation, the reporter hire, the tools, the pitch meetings. Releases from everyone on camera.
1. **Mystery:** the masked host appears before the show exists. Short "dear viewer" clips, no explanation. Clippers seed them.
2. **Pilot drop + the call:** "If you were in the room, talk to us." Recruiting whistleblowers becomes part of the plot.
3. **Escalation:** real reactions (denials, takedowns, legal letters, copycats) feed the host's arc.
4. **Media moment:** the host shows up somewhere real, in character (e.g., Basel around the BIS AGM, late June).
5. **The feature:** cut the series, the hunt, and the reactions into the meta movie.

**The one hard line:** the host is fiction, the testimony is real, and the audience must always be able to tell which is which. If the real stories get mixed up with the hoax (the Phoenix problem), the whistleblowers lose credibility, and so does everything else. Behind-the-scenes footage must never show a source's face, voice, location, or documents.
